# Aurelle Spaces — Editorial & Content Writing Style Guide

**Platform Client:** DRK Information Software Solutions  
**Purpose:** Establish authentic, professional Indian English guidelines for all architectural copywriting across the Aurelle Spaces platform.

---

## 1. Core Brand Voice & Tone

Aurelle Spaces communicates with the calm confidence, technical precision, and understated sophistication of an established Indian architecture and interior design studio.

### Key Tone Attributes

| Quality | In Practice | Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Architectural Precision** | Specific discussions of spatial layout, natural light, cross-ventilation, load-bearing materiality, and site topography. | Vague marketing jargon, hype, and empty adjectives ("stunning", "mind-blowing"). |
| **Understated Luxury** | Grounded descriptions of craftsmanship, custom joinery, natural stone finishes, and timeless longevity. | Flashy, loud consumer phrases ("cheap luxury", "affordable opulence", "mega-deals"). |
| **Authentic Indian Context** | Respectful integration of Indian climatic realities (monsoon detailing, verandas, courtyards, jaalis, passive solar shading). | Foreign architectural clichés divorced from regional conditions. |
| **Restrained Professionalism** | Objective explanations of architectural processes, feasibility reviews, and client consultations. | Exaggerated claims or fabricated social proof ("India's #1 studio", "World-class solutions"). |

---

## 2. Prohibited Vocabulary & Marketing Clichés

Never use hyperbolic marketing claims or generic SaaS buzzwords in public-facing studio copy:

- ❌ "India's No. 1 architecture studio"
- ❌ "Award-winning studio" (unless a specific accredited award with year is verified)
- ❌ "Trusted by thousands of clients"
- ❌ "Revolutionary design experience"
- ❌ "World-class solutions"
- ❌ "Unmatched excellence" / "Unparalleled perfection"
- ❌ "Cutting-edge AI-driven architecture" (keep the AI chatbot identified strictly as a virtual studio assistant without claiming the architecture itself is generated by algorithms)

---

## 3. Preferred Architectural Vocabulary

Use grounded, descriptive vocabulary that demonstrates genuine domain knowledge:

- **Spatial & Planning:** *Circulation, spatial sequence, courtyard typology, dual-aspect rooms, volumetric massing, sightlines, verandas, buffer zones, cross-ventilation, solar orientation.*
- **Materiality & Craft:** *Exposed basalt, polished Kota stone, fluted glass, brushed bronze hardware, oiled Burma teak, lime-plastered surfaces, monolithic cast terrazzo, woven brass accents.*
- **Processes & Execution:** *Topographical survey, structural coordination, turnkey execution, MEP coordination, climatic envelope, joinery detailing, statutory municipality approvals.*

---

## 4. Spelling & Linguistic Conventions

1. **British / Indian English:** Always follow standard Indian and British English conventions:
   - *Colour* (not color)
   - *Programme* (not program)
   - *Customise / Organise* (or customize/organize with standard British usage)
   - *Verandah / Veranda*
2. **Real Estate & Area Units:**
   - Always format built-up area in **sq. ft.** (e.g. *8,400 sq. ft.*) rather than square meters alone.
   - Use standard Indian typology terms when applicable (*bungalow, duplex, penthouse, apartment, workspace, plotted villa*).
3. **Numerals & Metrics:**
   - Write numbers below ten as words (*three courtyards, eight-metre cantilever*).
   - Use figures for measurements and areas (*4,200 sq. ft., 12 mm fluted glazing*).

---

## 5. Conversational Business Assistant Copy Policy

When the conversational AI assistant is implemented in Prompt 11:
- State clearly that it is the **Aurelle Spaces Virtual Studio Assistant**.
- Do not pretend it is a real human architect or partner.
- Its role is to guide prospective clients through services, answer studio questions, and assist in drafting project briefs for the human partners to review.
